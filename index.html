How this simulation works

What you control

  • Temperature (25–600 °C) changes both the equilibrium constant Kp and the rate at which the mixture moves toward equilibrium.
  • Pressure (1–300 atm, total) does not change Kp, but compresses the mixture so the reaction quotient Q changes and the mixture shifts toward the side with fewer moles of gas.
  • Add N2 / Add H2 / Remove NH3 change the amounts in the vessel; the mixture is then out of equilibrium and relaxes back.
  • Catalyst sets the activation energy shown on the Maxwell–Boltzmann plot and the speed of the approach to equilibrium. No catalyst: nothing happens on any human time-scale, at any temperature in range.
  • The simulation runs in real time: the "time to reach equilibrium" readout tells you how long the wait will be at the current settings.

Thermodynamics

ΔH°(T) and ΔS°(T) are computed at every temperature from the NIST-JANAF Shomate functions of N2, H2 and NH3; Kp = exp(−ΔG°/RT) with the 1 atm standard state used by the gauges. At 25 °C Kp ≈ 5.6×105; at 450 °C ≈ 5×10−5. The lower plot is the free energy of the whole mixture at fixed T and P as the reaction proceeds, drawn against log Q; its minimum is at log Kp, and the marker shows where the vessel is now.

Kinetics

The vessel relaxes toward its equilibrium composition with a first-order rate constant k(T) = kref·exp[−(Ea/R)(1/T − 1/723 K)]. Iron (promoted magnetite, the Haber–Bosch catalyst) is calibrated so that 95 % of the approach takes 20 s at 450 °C, a realistic converter residence time; its apparent activation energy of ≈70 kJ/mol comes from Temkin–Pyzhev and Somorjai's Fe(111) measurements. Ruthenium (Cs–Ru/MgO type) uses the literature apparent Ea ≈ 100 kJ/mol and is set 10× faster than iron at 450 °C, matching the 10–20× activity advantage reported for industrial Ru catalysts. "No catalyst" uses the computed gas-phase barrier of 524 kJ/mol (N2 + H2 → NNH2, Hwang & Mebel 2003); no homogeneous rate has ever been measured.

The Maxwell–Boltzmann curve is the true distribution of molecular kinetic energies at the set temperature. On a linear scale the fraction of molecules above 70 kJ/mol is invisible (about 1 in 105 at 450 °C); switch on the log scale to see the tail and how it grows with temperature. The table under the plot gives the exact fraction for each activation energy.
